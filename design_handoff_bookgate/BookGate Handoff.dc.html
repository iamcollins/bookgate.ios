BOOKGATE · DESIGN HANDOFF

BookGate — the reading-alarm app, as designed

Tokens, screen map and behaviour rules for the screens locked in design review, plus the open items that still need product decisions. Every screen referenced by id (4b, 11d) exists in the review document and is the source of truth for layout; this document is the source of truth for values and behaviour.

Status: core night flow, library, progress and onboarding locked · light theme complete · localisation sampled · prices, Settings root and trial-expired outstanding.

1 · What the app is

BookGate is an alarm for your book. At a set time each night the app rings, asks you to show it the book with the camera, shields the apps that usually win that hour, and runs a quiet timed session. Afterwards you record a thirty-second takeaway in your own voice. There is no free tier: a three-day trial, then a subscription.

Two rules shaped every screen. Nothing shames the user — a missed night is stated once, in plain words, with no red and no reset drama. And the word "reminder" never appears; it is an alarm, and it behaves like one.

6 · Behaviour rules

  • Session length is one app-wide setting, not per-book. Asked once at first open (5 minutes preselected), lives in Settings → Reading forever after. The nightly sheet changes tonight only.
  • The default earns its way up. After a clean week at the current length the step-up is offered once, immediately after a session, one increment only (5 → 10 → 15 → 20 → 30 → 45 → 60). Declining is a first-class choice and is never asked again that week.
  • A miss is stated once. No red, no counter-reset animation, no catch-up prompt. In the week row and heatmap a missed night is a hairline outline; days that haven't happened yet are drawn as nothing.
  • Heatmap fill weight is session length, never a score. Four steps. A short night still counts as a night.
  • Progress shows three facts — streak, the current month, total time. No averages, no projections, no "best day", no percentages.
  • One primary action per screen. Everything else is a quiet row or a text button. Emergency access on the shield is always present and never styled as a punishment.
  • Every button is a min-height box with centred wrapping text (min 44px, 48–58px typical). Never a fixed single-line height — see localisation.
  • Recording a takeaway is optional every single time. "Not now" carries no penalty and does not break the streak.
  • The word "reminder" never appears. Nor does any shaming or streak-loss language.

7 · Localisation

Reference screens: 12a–12d — session, shield, alarm and paywall, each in German and Japanese, with no layout changes made to accommodate them. German was chosen for length (30–40% longer, compounds that cannot hyphenate), Japanese for line-breaking and type.

  • Buttons must wrap, not shrink. German "10 Minuten später erinnern" fits at 15.5px only because the alarm's actions are full-width and stacked. Never place two buttons side by side on a decision screen.
  • Japanese type: Newsreader has no CJK. Titles fall back to Noto Serif JP, UI to Noto Sans JP, at the same sizes and weights. Set font-style: normal on CJK — synthetic oblique is not italic.
  • CJK breaks are authored. No spaces means auto-wrap breaks in the wrong place; titles and shield copy ship with manual break hints in the string.
  • Clocks: 24-hour with "Uhr" in German; Japanese drops the meridiem. Session readouts and heatmap numerals stay latin numerals in every locale, in the same font.
  • Prices localise format (29,99 € vs €29.99) but plan-card numerals never change font.
  • Where German cannot fit, shorten the string — do not shrink the type or grow the screen. The paywall needed exactly this twice (29,99 €/Jahr · 2,50 €/Monat, Danach 29,99 €/Jahr. Jederzeit kündbar.): at that density German needs shorter copy, and the layout is correct as designed.
  • What 12a–12d do not prove: that the copy is good. The German is translation-quality, not native, and the trial timeline is regulated wording. A native pass plus legal review is required per market before either locale ships — especially the trial, billing and cancellation strings.

8 · Open items — must be verified before build

  1. Prices are placeholders. Every number on 8f / 11l (€29.99 yearly, €5.99 monthly, SAVE 58%, €2.50 a month) is invented for layout. Replace with real store pricing per market, and re-derive the savings badge — it must be computed, never hard-coded. Confirm whether an intro offer or lifetime tier exists; neither is designed.
  2. Onboarding order and length need product sign-off. As designed: welcome → how it works → scan (skippable) → length → shielded apps → permissions → trial, with four numbered step dots covering scan/length/apps/permissions, and the paywall last, after setup. If a step is added, removed or reordered, the dots and their captions must be updated with it — they are drawn to match this exact sequence.
  3. All on-screen labels and copy are design copy. Titles, row labels, captions, empty-state sentences, permission explanations and legal footnotes should be reviewed and adjusted to final product and legal wording; several will change length and every block is built to flex, but re-check the four tightest screens (session, shield, alarm, paywall) after any edit.
  4. Trial-expired / resubscribe is not designed. Blocked on a policy decision: does a lapsed user get a hard wall (shield off, library read-only) or do sessions still run unshielded? With no free tier this screen decides whether lapsed users return.
  5. Settings root is not designed. Only the Reading sub-page (6a) exists; the three dots currently open it directly, and its back chevron has nowhere to go.
  6. Permission-denial paths. Each permission is requested separately so a refusal doesn't kill the flow, but the degraded states (no camera → manual start only; no notifications → no alarm) are described, not drawn.
  7. Real assets. Every cover is a typographic placeholder and every shielded app is a striped tile; both are drop-in replacements that change no layout.
  8. Accessibility. Dynamic Type, VoiceOver labels for the bookmark/heatmap graphics, and reduced-motion behaviour for the drifting ambient (freeze, don't remove) are unspecified.

9 · The prototype

Screen 1a in the review document is clickable and covers the whole app: tab bar, night flow, library push, onboarding sequence, step-up, year strip and Settings. Its rail jumps to any screen and speeds the session clock. Four switches are exposed: session length, missed-yesterday Today, already-read Today, and clean-week (which reveals the step-up after a session). Inert by design: Progress's month steppers and the individual Settings rows.
